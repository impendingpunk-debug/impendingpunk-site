WEEKEND RENTAL / NINTENDO 64

The GoldenEye
Heist

One new video store. Two underage agents. One absolutely foolproof plan.

One of my favorite old memories is riding with my brother, Jason, and our dad to the Blockbuster that had just opened in Belleview, Florida.

It was the weekend, which meant family movies and—if we were lucky—a game or two. But Jason and I already knew what we wanted. Dad probably knew what we were going to try to get, too.

GoldenEye 007.

By then, the game had already been out for a couple of years. But before that store opened, we hadn’t really had anywhere nearby to rent games. The games we knew were the ones Mom had bought for us to own: Zelda. Mario Kart. Games that lived beside the Nintendo.

We both wanted GoldenEye. There was only one problem: we were way too young to be playing it.

So, naturally, we came up with a plan. Jason grabbed some random game and we put it on top of GoldenEye. I carried both boxes up to Dad, apparently believing the bottom one had become invisible.

Dad looked at the games. And rented both.

THE PERFECT CRIME!
(or so we thought)

At the time, we thought we’d pulled off the greatest scheme in history. Now that I’m a dad, I’m pretty sure he knew exactly what we were doing.

Either way, we got GoldenEye. Jason and I spent that weekend playing a Rated M game years before either of us had any business playing it.

More than twenty-five years later, it’s still one of my most cherished memories. Not just because GoldenEye was a great game. I remember the ride with Dad. The excitement of that new store. Plotting with my brother. Walking out with the game. Knowing exactly how we would spend the weekend.

It’s strange which moments survive. Sometimes it’s a car ride. A video store. A Nintendo cartridge. Your brother beside you. And a weekend that, at the time, felt completely ordinary.

SIDE B / THE BIGGER STORY

Welcome to the ’90s.
And welcome to my life.

FRIDAY NIGHT VIDEO STORESCARTRIDGES LIKE TREASURESATURDAY MORNING CARTOONSDIAL-UP INTERNETMALLS + ARCADESTHE BACK SEAT, WHILE OUR PARENTS HANDLED THE WORLD

We didn’t know any of it was temporary. We didn’t know the video store would disappear, the games we fought over would become collectibles, or that ordinary weekends would become memories we’d give anything to visit again.

We were just kids. Somehow, the world we grew up in became history.

This site is my attempt to hold onto it—the games, the stores, the technology, the places, the people, and the dumb little moments that mattered more than we realized. Some of it is the story of a generation. Some of it is just Jack’s.

And one day, when my daughter wants to know what the world looked like before hers, I hope this helps me show her.
